# Festschrift Framing and Article Outline 01

## Recommendation in One Sentence

Write a genuine research essay with a personal frame: open with the doctoral question Michael helped Karsten formulate, carry that question into agentic BPM, make a new conceptual contribution, and close by showing why the question remains a fitting part of Michael's intellectual legacy.

## What a Festschrift Is

`Festschrift` literally means a celebratory publication. In the German academic tradition, colleagues, collaborators, and former students contribute work honoring a scholar, often at a milestone. The strongest contributions are not testimonials padded into papers. They are scholarly gifts connected to the honoree's interests, influence, or way of thinking.

Computer-science Festschriften published in LNCS commonly mix biographical or reminiscence material in the volume's front matter with refereed research chapters. Individual chapters therefore tend to remain recognizable academic articles. Their commemorative character appears through problem choice, intellectual lineage, a brief personal connection, and the framing of the contribution.

For this chapter, the right balance is approximately:

- 10 percent personal and commemorative framing;
- 75 percent scholarly reconstruction and conceptual argument;
- 15 percent research agenda and reflective conclusion.

Those proportions are editorial guidance, not a mechanical word-count rule.

## Expected Tone

Aim for:

- warm but not ceremonial;
- grateful but not hagiographic;
- reflective but not nostalgic;
- intellectually generous but willing to state unresolved problems;
- personal in the introduction and conclusion, analytical in the body.

Avoid:

- a biography of Michael;
- a list of his publications or achievements;
- repeated adjectives such as `seminal`, `visionary`, or `transformative`;
- claims about private conversations that Karsten has not confirmed;
- treating the chapter as an obituary or retirement speech;
- making the article's current-AI section a general technology survey.

The tribute should arise from the argument. Michael's early context-aware BPM work widened the process boundary to include environmental drivers. His recent account of process autonomization again asks BPM to widen its frame. Karsten's updated CAIS logic can connect those moments.

*Context Is More Than a Window: From Context-Aware Information Systems to Agentic Business Processes*

### Alternatives

### Article type

Conceptual synthesis and research agenda with reflective Festschrift framing.

It should not claim to be:

- a systematic literature review;
- an empirical evaluation;
- a validated extension of the original design theory;

## Core Question and Answer

**Question:** What can the design theory for context-aware information systems contribute to the design and governance of contemporary agentic business processes?

**Answer:** Agentic AI makes the old context problem executable but does not solve it. A context window can contain representations of a situation, while context-awareness requires the surrounding socio-technical system to select relevant signals, relate them to goals, choose authorized and timely adaptations, and learn from outcomes under governance.

## Article Contribution

The manuscript should make three explicit moves:

1. **Reconstruct:** recover the five CAIS meta-requirements and their socio-technical assumptions from the 2013 thesis.
2. **Differentiate:** explain what is new in language agents, including generative planning, tool use, iterative observation, memory, and possible self-modification.
3. **Translate:** propose five updated design principles and use them to define a research agenda for context-aware agentic process systems.

The new contribution is the translation. The historical and current literature establish the two ends of the bridge, but the bridge itself is this article's conceptual synthesis.

## Proposed Structure

### Abstract, 130-170 words

- Name the historical and present problem.
- State the context-window distinction.
- Describe the method as a conceptual reconstruction and synthesis.
- State the five-principle result and research contribution.
- Do not include citations.

### 1 Introduction: A Question That Returned

- Begin with the doctorate under Michael Rosemann and Jan Recker while Karsten worked full time.
- Use the verified `rocky road to completion` phrase once and lightly.
- Explain that a question once framed around context-aware information systems has returned through AI agents able to act on their environments.
- State the research question, conceptual method, contribution, and article roadmap.
- Insert one concise, publication-cleared personal memory when supplied.

### 2 Before Agency: Moving Context Into the Process Frame

- Reconstruct the BPM problem in 2008-2011.
- Contrast intrinsic process flexibility with extrinsic drivers.
- Explain the joint work and cases without over-narrating the thesis.
- Establish process as a complex adaptive, socio-technical system.

### 3 The CAIS Design Theory

- Define system, context, and adaptation.
- Explain the three research questions.
- Present the five meta-requirements in a compact table.
- State the analytical evaluation and its limits.

### 4 What Agentic AI Changes

- Define an agent operationally as a system that pursues goals through environment-facing actions and incorporates resulting observations.
- Use ReAct to illustrate iterative reasoning and acting.
- Use Reflexion to illustrate feedback memory.
- Use AgentBench to establish limitations.
- Draw the critical distinction between a context window and context-awareness.

### 5 From AI-Augmented BPM to Agentic BPM

- Present the ABPMS lifecycle and characteristics.
- Use Large Process Models as a hybrid generative and symbolic vision.
- Discuss Rosemann et al.'s transaction/conversation, automation/autonomization, and simplification/sophistication drifts.
- Introduce the 2026 APM manifesto and explicit process frames.

### 6 Five Principles for Context-Aware Agentic Process Systems

Start with one mapping table, then give each principle a short subsection:

1. Frame the socio-technical system and its goals.
2. Select and maintain relevant context.
3. Provide sufficient but bounded action variety.
4. Match situations to actions with traceability and escalation.
5. Learn through governed change.

Each subsection should state continuity, what is technically new, and a design implication.

### 7 What Changed and What Did Not

Use a compact comparison:

- Changed: probabilistic generation, open-ended plan construction, tool calls, unstructured observations, natural-language interfaces, multi-agent interaction, and run-time self-modification.
- Persistent: goals, boundaries, context relevance, socio-technical effects, requisite variety, response timing, responsibility, learning validity, and evaluation in use.

This section prevents a simplistic claim that a 2013 framework predicted or already specified modern agents.

### 8 Research Agenda

Organize around five empirically investigable areas:

- context boundary and provenance;
- bounded action variety and guardrails;
- activation under uncertainty and latency;
- governed learning and rollback;
- evaluation of socio-technical outcomes.

### 9 Conclusion: Context as a Way of Seeing

- Restate the conceptual answer.
- Return to Michael's influence without generic praise.
- Suggest that the lasting lesson is to ask what lies outside the model yet changes whether the process can achieve its purpose.
- Keep the closing warm and restrained.

## Recommended Personal Frame

The current evidence supports this opening logic:

> More than a decade ago, while completing a doctorate at QUT under Michael Rosemann and Jan Recker and working full time, I asked what an information system must know about its environment to adapt effectively. In my acknowledgments, I thanked them for guiding me along the "rocky road to completion." Time has turned that road into an unexpectedly direct route to a current question: what must an AI agent know, and how should it be governed, when it can act on the world it observes?

This is a working passage, not fixed text. One concrete memory would be stronger than the published acknowledgment alone. Ideal details are modest and intellectual: a question Michael repeatedly asked, a diagram he challenged, a reframing that changed the research, or a moment when he connected the work to a broader BPM problem.

## Contribution Language

Use:

- `This article reconstructs...`
- `I argue...`
- `I propose five design principles...`
- `The mapping offers a conceptual synthesis and research agenda...`
- `These principles require empirical evaluation...`

Avoid:

- `This study proves...`
- `The framework guarantees...`
- `Modern agents implement the CAIS theory...`
- `The original theory anticipated all features of agentic AI...`
