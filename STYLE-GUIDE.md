# Michael Festschrift Style Guide

## Document Shape

- Use `# Michael Festschrift` as the title for main planning files unless the user confirms another working title.
- Use `##` headings for major sections.
- Keep planning documents compact and easy to scan.
- Separate source material from analysis, drafts, archives, and final outputs.
- Use versioned filenames when draft iterations begin to branch.

## Folder Structure

- Keep steering and project-management files in the root folder.
- Keep originals and intermediary conversions in `raw/`.
- Keep read-only normalized source materials and reference notes in `sources/`.
- Keep scope, contributor planning, thematic analysis, permissions, fact checking, and editorial synthesis in `analysis/`.
- Keep editable outreach, contributor guidance, manuscript sections, captions, and working notes in `drafts/`.
- Keep superseded draft snapshots in `archive/`.
- Keep generated review and publication exports in `output/`.
- Create `media/` only when approved visual assets require a dedicated working area.
- Use lowercase folder names.
- Use uppercase base filenames with lowercase file extensions for steering, analysis, source, and draft files.
- Use lowercase filenames for generated files in `output/`.

## Editorial Voice

- Prefer concrete memories and specific observations over generic praise.
- Preserve the contributor's natural voice while improving clarity and consistency.
- Keep warmth without exaggeration or invented intimacy.
- Avoid flattening distinct contributions into a single institutional voice.
- Use the smallest amount of editorial framing needed to help the collection cohere.

## Attribution and Accuracy

- Do not invent facts, quotations, dates, relationships, or permissions.
- Distinguish direct quotation, paraphrase, and editorial synthesis.
- Record the source for factual claims, quotations, images, and captions.
- Mark unresolved details with clear placeholders.
- Verify names, titles, dates, spellings, captions, and credits before publication.

## Contributor Editing

- Preserve meaning when copyediting.
- Flag substantive edits for contributor or project-owner review.
- Keep a clean current draft and archive superseded versions when changes become material.
- Record whether a submission is received, edited, approved, and cleared for publication.

## Privacy and Permissions

- Treat private correspondence and unpublished stories as private working material by default.
- Do not expose personal contact details in shareable outputs.
- Track permission for text, photographs, artwork, and substantial quotations.
- Confirm the intended distribution level before preparing externally shareable exports.

## Formatting Rules

- Use simple Markdown.
- Use one blank line between headings, paragraphs, and lists.
- Prefer short sentences and concrete language.
- Keep contributor names and credits consistent with their confirmed preferences.
- Use placeholders for unresolved names, dates, links, credits, captions, and publication details.
- No em dashes.
