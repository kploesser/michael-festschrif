# Michael Rosemann Festschrift Article Style Guide

## Scholarly Festschrift Tone

- Treat the chapter as an intellectual gift: advance a question connected to Michael's work rather than merely praising it.
- Keep the scholarly contribution primary. Use personal reflection to explain why this question matters and why Michael is part of its lineage.
- Aim for warmth, gratitude, intellectual generosity, and candor.
- Avoid a biographical survey, generic superlatives, ceremonial excess, or an obituary-like tone.
- Use first person where Karsten is reflecting on his research, decisions, or relationship to the topic.
- Use third person and normal scholarly attribution for literature claims.
- Open with a specific connection to the doctoral work and close by returning to Michael's influence on how the field frames problems.

## Article Shape

- Use a concise title that names the conceptual movement from context-awareness to agentic processes.
- Provide a self-contained abstract and four to six keywords.
- State the article's question and contribution in the introduction.
- Reconstruct the 2013 design theory before describing current AI.
- Distinguish continuity from discontinuity. Do not describe agentic AI as a simple linear implementation of CAIS.
- Include one compact conceptual mapping between the five CAIS meta-requirements and current agentic-system concerns.
- Present new design principles as a proposed synthesis and research agenda, not as validated findings.
- End with both a scholarly conclusion and a restrained personal return to Michael.

## Core Argument

- A larger LLM context window is not context-awareness.
- Context-awareness is a socio-technical design achievement, not a property conferred by model scale.
- Agentic systems make context selection, action governance, adaptation, and learning operational at run time.
- The five CAIS meta-requirements remain useful, but their implementation must be updated for probabilistic models, tool use, autonomous action, and self-modification.
- BPM contributes the process frame through which agent autonomy can be bounded, explained, monitored, and improved.

## Terminology

- Define `context` as information about environmental or system conditions that is relevant to achieving work-system goals.
- Distinguish `context` from an LLM's `context window`.
- Use `context-aware information system (CAIS)` for the 2013 class.
- Use `AI-augmented BPM system (ABPMS)` for Dumas et al.'s 2023 formulation.
- Use `agentic business process management (APM)` for the 2026 manifesto's formulation.
- Use `agentic system` for the broader class of systems that can pursue goals through environment-facing actions.
- Prefer `bounded autonomy`, `framed autonomy`, or `governed adaptation` to unqualified `autonomy` in organizational settings.
- Avoid anthropomorphic verbs unless the system behavior is precisely defined.

## Evidence and Attribution

- Cite the thesis by page when working in Markdown; convert to the final LNCS reference style during typesetting.
- Use primary publications for technical claims.
- Separate direct evidence from inference using phrases such as `the thesis established`, `later work proposed`, and `this article argues`.
- Do not claim that contemporary systems satisfy the CAIS design theory merely because they use tools or feedback.
- Do not attribute ideas to Michael without a publication or Karsten's confirmation.
- Use short quotations only when the original wording matters.

## LNCS Conventions

- Use American English.
- Use numeric citations in square brackets.
- Include DOI or stable primary-source links where available.
- Number the first two levels of headings in the final artifact.
- Position table captions above tables and figure captions below figures.
- Make tables editable rather than embedding them as images.
- Supply alt text for figures and other non-text content.
- Keep an abstract self-contained and free of citations.
- Treat generic 12 to 15 page guidance as provisional until the volume editors' instructions are available.
- Include a disclosure-of-interests statement and any required AI-assistance disclosure in the final version.

## Formatting Rules

- Use clear Markdown for working files.
- Use one blank line between headings, paragraphs, and lists.
- Prefer short paragraphs and explicit transitions.
- Use title case for manuscript headings when preparing the LNCS artifact.
- Use sentence case for working-document headings unless the heading is copied from the manuscript.
- Use placeholders only for facts the author must supply, and label them `AUTHOR INPUT`.
- No em dashes.
